# DM lore

Use this reference for bestiary lore, adventure background, and hidden information separated from a player-facing passage. This is polished sourcebook prose for the DM, not read-aloud text and not a substitute for mechanics.

## Lore contract

DM lore explains what a thing is, why it is this way, how it behaves, and what consequences follow. It may contain information unavailable to the characters, but every claim must come from the source or an explicitly authorized lore expansion.

Write with bounded authority:

- state established facts directly;
- mark rumor, uncertainty, disagreement, and in-world belief as such;
- preserve the source's degree of certainty;
- distinguish a common pattern from an absolute rule;
- connect cause to visible consequence where the source supports the connection.

Do not make a passage sound authoritative by inventing dates, proper names, quotations, factions, motives, or cosmology.

## Bestiary lore

Select details that help the DM portray or place the creature:

- **Identity:** what kind of creature it is and the feature that distinguishes it.
- **Origin:** how it came to exist, when the source establishes this.
- **Habitat and signs:** where it dwells and what evidence marks its presence.
- **Behavior:** what it wants, avoids, protects, hunts, or repeats.
- **Relationships:** how it treats prey, rivals, servants, communities, or its environment.
- **Consequence:** what changes when it settles in a place or acts unchecked.
- **Table relevance:** what characters might witness, learn, bargain over, or oppose.

Use only the dimensions supported by the source. Do not force every entry into the same template. Avoid paraphrasing the stat block: lore should explain play rather than recite mechanics.

A creature becomes memorable through a specific pattern, not a pile of sinister adjectives. Prefer “It wedges the bones of prior kills into boundary trees” over an unsupported claim that it is “a terrifying and malevolent predator.”

## Adventure lore

Adventure lore gives the DM causal context:

1. what happened or is happening;
2. who or what caused it;
3. why the present location, conflict, or clue has its current form;
4. what changes if no one intervenes.

Organize by the relationship the DM needs to understand, not necessarily by chronology. Keep actionable facts near the location, creature, or event they govern. Preserve secret information as DM-facing material rather than laundering it into boxed prose.

Operational instructions should remain direct. A trigger, check, DC, damage value, duration, condition, or branching consequence belongs under **Mechanics**, even when surrounded by lore.

## Diction and structure

Use a sourcebook register that is assured, compact, and concrete.

- Start with the defining claim, event, or relationship.
- Let each paragraph develop one idea.
- Prefer concrete causes and practices to abstract claims about nature or destiny.
- Use fantasy terminology consistently, but do not add setting-specific names absent from the source.
- Use metaphor to clarify one idea, then return to literal exposition.
- End on a consequence, behavior, or usable tension when the source contains one.

Avoid:

- repeated “not merely X, but Y” constructions;
- empty scale claims such as “since time immemorial”;
- universal “always” and “never” statements unsupported by the source;
- moral labels standing in for motives or behavior;
- promotional phrases promising untold secrets, epic danger, or unforgettable adventure;
- faux quotations and legends added only to simulate history;
- second-person instructions hidden inside lore paragraphs.

## Relationship to read-aloud prose

DM lore can explain the meaning of a player-facing detail, but the two passages should not duplicate each other sentence by sentence.

Example division:

- **Read Aloud:** the western wall bears a clean, door-shaped patch amid centuries of soot.
- **DM Lore:** the former doorway was sealed after the prior abbot trapped a revenant in the adjoining crypt.
- **Mechanics:** a character who searches the wall finds the seam with a successful DC supplied by the source.

The read-aloud passage presents evidence. DM lore supplies the hidden cause. Mechanics govern discovery and consequence.

## DM-lore gate

Verify that:

- every claim is sourced or permitted expansion;
- uncertainty and in-world belief remain marked;
- the prose gives the DM usable understanding rather than ornamental history;
- bestiary lore explains behavior and consequence instead of restating statistics;
- hidden facts remain outside player-facing prose;
- mechanics remain exact and separately identifiable.
