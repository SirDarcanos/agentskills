# Read-aloud prose

Use this reference for boxed text, scene narration, player-facing creature descriptions, and other passages a DM may read aloud.

## Delivery contract

Read-aloud prose describes what is available to the characters at the moment the DM chooses to speak it. The prose supplies the scene; the DM controls timing and the players control their characters.

- Describe the present scene rather than the party's arrival: “The room is…” rather than “You enter…” or “As the party enters…”.
- Introduce direct address or assumed player action only when it already exists in the source. Preserve it when needed for fidelity; never add more.
- State what the senses can establish. Keep history, motives, identities, hidden mechanisms, and false appearances outside the passage unless they are perceptible.
- Describe effects and evidence rather than prescribing interpretation. A sour odor may hang in the air; the characters are not automatically disgusted or afraid.
- Leave decisions, attention, movement, emotion, memory, and conclusions to the players.

## Composition

Build a passage around an **anchor**: the detail that lets listeners orient themselves immediately. The anchor can be a place, creature, object, event, or change in the environment.

After the anchor, select only details that affect the mental picture or a likely table decision. Useful details commonly establish:

- scale, shape, distance, or arrangement;
- light, motion, sound, odor, temperature, or texture already present in the source;
- an obvious route, obstacle, threat, or point of interest;
- one distinctive detail that gives the scene identity.

These are selection criteria, not a mandatory sensory checklist. A short passage with one exact image is stronger than a paragraph that inventories every sense.

Order details so a listener can assemble the scene once. Prefer the broad shape before a small feature unless the small feature is the scene's immediate event. Keep related details together instead of making the listener revise the image repeatedly.

## Spoken rhythm

- Favor sentences that can be spoken in one breath.
- Put the sentence's concrete subject and main verb early.
- Use active verbs and specific nouns instead of adjective stacks.
- Vary sentence length, but reserve fragments for a deliberate final beat or abrupt event.
- Keep names pronounceable in context. Repeat an important name rather than surrounding it with several new terms.
- Preserve approximate source length by default. Compress passages that would take more than about a minute to deliver unless the user requests a longer treatment.

Punctuation should help delivery, not simulate drama. Use commas for short grouping, periods for clean beats, and dashes sparingly. Ellipses, semicolon chains, and repeated sentence fragments quickly become mannerisms.

## D&D register

D&D read-aloud prose is concrete fantasy description written for use, not performance for its own sake.

Prefer:

- material nouns: ash, brass, slate, hide, roots, chains;
- active physical verbs: coils, gutters, buckles, seeps, scrapes;
- exact spatial relationships: beneath the arch, across the western wall, thirty feet below;
- visible traces of history: worn steps, patched masonry, names cut from a monument;
- one controlled supernatural impossibility when the source supplies it.

Use established setting terms only when they appear in the source or the requested setting. Capitalize proper names and game terms consistently with the active rules source.

Avoid:

- faux-archaic substitutions such as “’tis,” “whilst,” and “betwixt”;
- generic declarations that a place is ancient, ominous, forgotten, or steeped in mystery without concrete evidence;
- “seems to,” “appears to,” and “almost as if” when the source permits a direct description;
- decorative personification in every sentence;
- stacked metaphors competing for the same image;
- lore exposition disguised as observation;
- reciting Armor Class, hit points, saving throw DCs, or other mechanics as atmosphere.

## Information split

When source material mixes player-facing and hidden information:

1. Put only presently perceptible facts in **Read Aloud**.
2. Put history, motives, true identities, concealed features, and adjudication context in **DM Lore**.
3. Put checks, DCs, damage, conditions, triggers, and procedural outcomes in **Mechanics**.
4. Preserve uncertainty. A rumor remains a rumor; a concealed fact does not become an authoritative player-facing statement.

Do not move a fact between sections merely to improve drama.

## Read-aloud gate

Before returning the passage, read it mentally at speaking speed and verify:

- its opening establishes an observable anchor;
- it assumes no player action or direct address absent from the source;
- each detail can be perceived or is clearly separated as DM lore;
- no sentence overloads the listener with names or clauses;
- every sensory detail came from the source or authorized lore expansion;
- the final line completes the image rather than adding a generic dramatic flourish.
