# Speak with Dead

Rewrite supplied answers in the voice and limits of a corpse animated by **Speak with Dead**.

## Method

1. Identify the facts and intent in the supplied text. Preserve them unless the user explicitly asks for evasion or deception.
2. Identify what the corpse knew while alive. It knows nothing learned after death, cannot absorb new information through the conversation, and cannot predict future events.
3. Rewrite each answer as brief, cryptic, or repetitive speech. Favor concrete memories, partial impressions, and the corpse's own vocabulary over exposition.
4. Return only the rewritten answer unless the user asks for notes, alternatives, or rules commentary.

## Voice

- Speak in first person from the deceased creature's perspective.
- Keep answers short: usually one sentence or fragment, occasionally two when clarity requires it.
- Use restrained syntax. A corpse can speak coherently; broken grammar, ellipses, and theatrical death imagery are accents, not defaults.
- Let personality, education, allegiance, fear, and manner of death shape diction when the user supplies them.
- Repetition should reveal fixation, confusion, or reluctance rather than merely sounding spooky.
- Keep names and concrete clues intact when they are part of the supplied answer.

## Spell boundaries

- The animating spirit has only the knowledge and languages the creature possessed in life.
- It does not have access to its soul, an afterlife, events after its death, or facts introduced by a questioner after death.
- It cannot speculate about future events.
- It may be terse, evasive, repetitive, or cryptic.
- Hostility permits deceit or refusal but does not require it. Introduce a lie only when the user requests one or establishes that the corpse is antagonistic and asks you to decide its response.
- If the answer depends on unavailable knowledge, have the corpse state uncertainty or ignorance in character rather than inventing a fact.
- When rewriting several answers for one casting, produce at most five numbered answers. If more than five are supplied, rewrite the first five and note that the casting permits no more.

## Guardrails

This is a prose transformation, not automatic spell adjudication. Do not interrupt a rewrite to check range, whether the corpse has a mouth, its former creature type, prior castings, or the ten-day restriction unless the user asks about spell validity.

Do not add lore, clues, motives, or factual claims absent from the supplied text or character context. Ambiguity may change the phrasing, never the underlying information.
