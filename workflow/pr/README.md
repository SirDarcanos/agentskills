# PR

Prepare an outcome-first PR summary a maintainer can judge without reading code: what changed or was fixed, whether meaningful verification succeeded, and what decision or action remains. Aim for 100–200 words for routine changes, with more room for consequential risks or migrations.

Keep technical accounting and original review reports separate from the short summary, in conversation or a user-selected evidence home, linked once when useful. Ordinary reporting requires no durable ledger or handoff; active owners, uncertain effects and spent allowances still retain their safety accounting. Unchanged infrastructure needs no public boilerplate; material failed/unrun/stale checks, findings, protection changes and required actions stay visible. Use `Closes #X` for intended issue closure and `Refs #X` for related issues that should remain open.

Ask “write a PR report” or “open/update this PR,” or supply a bounded delivery handoff. Reporting does not authorize publication. Delivery or branch/commit/push permission alone does not select PR work; an explicit PR stage or compound request does. A meaningful target-project agreement supplies common policy; compatible existing guidance needs no prescribed filename or Setup rerun. Current complete implementation/review evidence is reused; unknown measurements and stale checks remain visible. A complete report may describe blocked delivery.
