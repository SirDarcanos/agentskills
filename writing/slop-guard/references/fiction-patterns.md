# Fiction patterns

Read this reference only for fiction or narrative prose. These patterns become defects when they replace character-specific perception, psychology, action, or consequence. A single familiar construction may be harmless; repeated defaults flatten a story.

## Vague interiority

The narration announces an internal change without identifying it.

**Signals:** an unnamed feeling shifts, breaks, tightens, flickers, or settles somewhere in the body.

**Test:** What changed in the character's thought, desire, expectation, or behavior?

**Fix:** Name the realization, show the physical experience precisely, reveal the resulting choice, or cut the beat.

## Stock physical reactions

Characters share the same small library of clenched jaws, caught breaths, racing hearts, tightened chests, rigid shoulders, and white knuckles.

**Why it fails:** The body language identifies generic intensity rather than this character's response.

**Fix:** Choose an observable action shaped by the character's history, coping style, surroundings, and immediate objective. Keep ordinary bodily reactions when their ordinariness is accurate and not repetitive.

## Atmosphere acting in place of characters

Silence stretches, rooms hold their breath, tension fills the air, or a city broods while the characters do nothing consequential.

**Fix:** Show who notices the pause, what they avoid, who breaks it, and what changes because of it. Let setting matter through point of view and action.

## Cinematic establishing filler

A scene opens on weather, skyline, architecture, or decorative light before locating the character's concern.

**Test:** Could the opening play over a slow camera pan without changing the story?

**Fix:** Begin where character, setting, and pressure meet. Keep environmental openings when the setting changes the available choices or embodies the point of view.

## Choreographed sequencing

Actions arrive as a chain of stage directions with identical weight: one gesture, then another, followed by a pause.

**Fix:** Compress routine motion. Expand only the action whose timing, resistance, or consequence matters.

## Performed hesitation

The prose repeatedly defines a reaction by denying it: a smile that is not a smile, anger that is not quite anger, a statement framed as not a question.

**Fix:** Commit to the character's mixed motive or describe what an observer can actually perceive. Keep negation when the failed category matters to the point of view.

## Proxy communication

The narrator claims that a look says everything, the characters need no words, or one person simply knows what another means.

**Fix:** Give the reader enough gesture, history, dialogue, or interior inference to understand the exchange. Ambiguity may remain, but it must be legible.

## Generic desire

A character wants, needs, craves, or aches for someone without a specific object beyond possession or proximity.

**Fix:** Identify what the character seeks from this person, why now, what contradiction complicates it, and what the desire may cost.

## Decorative damage

Sleeplessness, drinking, cigarettes, scars, or burnout serve as instant evidence of depth or danger.

**Fix:** Dramatize the practical and relational cost. Remove the detail when it functions only as styling.

## Competence shorthand

A character acts with effortless, clinical, practiced, surgical, or economical precision instead of performing a describable skill.

**Fix:** Show the decision, movement, trade-off, or overlooked detail that demonstrates competence. Distinguish confidence from infallibility.

## Default emotional metaphors

Attraction becomes gravity or magnetism; shock becomes a punch; emotion becomes a wave; restraint becomes steel; a voice becomes velvet.

**Fix:** Use imagery rooted in this character's experience and the scene's sensory world, or describe the effect directly. A familiar metaphor can stay when the point of view would genuinely choose it.

## Simulated lyricism

Parallel sentences, noun triplets, repeated fragments, or echo lines create a poetic cadence without developing the thought.

**Test:** Does each beat escalate, complicate, contradict, or reveal something new?

**Fix:** Cut the echo, combine the ideas, or make each beat perform distinct narrative work.

## Borrowed profundity

A character or narrator produces a polished aphorism about love, pain, survival, stories, or fate that could belong to any book.

**Fix:** Replace the universal slogan with the character's concrete thought in this moment. Keep quotable language when the character has earned and would plausibly use it.

## Mis-scaled intensity

A small gesture changes everything, the world stops, or part of the character breaks forever before the story establishes that scale.

**Fix:** Match the language to the demonstrated consequence. Reserve irreversible language for irreversible events.

## Narrated interpretation

An action is followed by an explanation of what it highlights, symbolizes, reflects, or proves.

**Fix:** Strengthen the action, move the interpretation into genuine point-of-view thought, or trust the reader and cut it.

## Name avoidance

The narration cycles through demographic descriptors, roles, and physical traits to avoid repeating a character's name.

**Fix:** Repeat the name or pronoun. Use a descriptor only when it reveals point of view, distance, recognition, or a changed relationship.

## Suspended endings

A scene ends with stillness, a hanging question, generalized atmosphere, or a summary of the emotion that just occurred.

**Fix:** End on a changed choice, concrete consequence, charged action, or specific unresolved pressure. Quiet endings still need movement in the story's state.

## Fiction finish checks

Before returning fiction, verify:

- internal states are specific enough to affect behavior;
- physical reactions differ by character and situation;
- setting enters through point of view or consequence;
- dialogue and gestures make subtext available to the reader;
- metaphors belong to the character and sensory world;
- rhythmic emphasis adds development rather than repetition;
- emotional scale matches what the scene has earned;
- the ending changes or sharpens the story's pressure;
- revision preserved deliberate voice, ambiguity, and stylistic roughness.
